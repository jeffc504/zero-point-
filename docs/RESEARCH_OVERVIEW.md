# Master Research Overview

## Research objective

Investigate whether controlled boundary conditions, electromagnetic structures, advanced materials, vacuum environments, and sensitive instrumentation can produce measurable quantum-vacuum phenomena—particularly Casimir and dynamical-Casimir effects—and determine experimentally whether any observed output is fully explained by supplied drive energy and known physical mechanisms.

## Research family A: VORTEX CORE ZERO-1

The earlier CORE ZERO-1 concept uses a toroidal vacuum-chamber architecture with electromagnetic excitation, stabilization structures, capacitor-based electrical buffering/capture, PLC or microcontroller sequencing, field sensing, thermal feedback, and isolated load measurement.

For open research, the valuable idea is the **modular toroidal experimental platform** rather than any assertion of guaranteed energy production.

Suggested experimental decomposition:

1. Characterize the toroidal structure electromagnetically without vacuum.
2. Characterize it under controlled pressure.
3. Measure coil input, reactive power, losses, thermal rise, and induced fields.
4. Test stabilization structures independently.
5. Characterize capacitor charging paths with all external inputs instrumented.
6. Use isolated dummy loads and redundant power analyzers.
7. Compare powered, unpowered, detuned, and sham configurations.

## Research family B: Graphene-enhanced Casimir platform

The later architecture explores:

- static Casimir measurements;
- dynamical-Casimir effects;
- planar and toroidal cavity geometries;
- graphene and graphene/hBN boundary/interface concepts;
- controlled boundary modulation;
- cryogenic environments;
- ultra-high-vacuum environments;
- SQUID and superconducting detector concepts;
- low-noise photon/field detection;
- parametric amplification as an experimental technique;
- energy harvesting/capture only as a measured downstream subsystem;
- rigorous closed energy accounting.

## Why toroidal geometry is interesting

A toroidal geometry provides a closed-loop experimental boundary, supports distributed sensing, permits multiple field-control orientations, and creates a useful comparison against conventional planar structures. Claims of superior performance remain hypotheses until experimentally demonstrated.

## Graphene research hypothesis

Graphene or graphene/hBN structures may be evaluated as tunable conductive, dielectric-interface, thermal, or quantum-material elements. Every graphene configuration should be compared against an otherwise-equivalent conventional control.

## Measurement-first philosophy

The platform should be treated as an instrument designed to answer a question, not as a generator assumed to work.

A credible anomaly requires:
- repeatability;
- independent metrology;
- complete accounting of all electrical/mechanical/thermal inputs;
- controls;
- uncertainty analysis;
- raw-data publication;
- independent replication.
